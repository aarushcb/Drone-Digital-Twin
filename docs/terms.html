1. What the app is, and what it is not

DroneTwinLabs reads the flight logs you upload (PX4 .ulg and ArduPilot .bin), takes measurements from them, and compares each flight with your drone's earlier flights, or with published PX4 and ArduPilot guidance until it has enough history. It reports what changed, where, and how confident it is.

It is not an airworthiness assessment, an inspection or a certification. Nothing in the app is a statement that a drone is safe to fly, fit for a purpose, or compliant with any regulation.

  • A good score is not a safety clearance. A high health score means only that the measurements the app can take look like your drone's normal. Many faults don't show up in those measurements at all.
  • A poor score is not a diagnosis. It means something measured has changed. The likely causes the app lists are suggestions to check, not findings of fact.
  • Findings are probabilistic and can be wrong in both directions. The app compares measurements against statistical baselines. It can flag a healthy drone (a false alarm) and it can miss a real problem (a missed detection). Its confidence labels say how much history stands behind each finding, not how likely the drone is to fail.
  • Early reads are weaker. Until your drone has about five flights in the same setup, findings are compared with general published guidance rather than your drone's own history.

2. What the app cannot tell you

These are limits of the data and methods, not features waiting to be switched on. The app never claims any of them, and you shouldn't read them into its results:

  • Remaining life of motors, props or the battery. There is no wear model to predict it from.
  • Which exact part failed. A change can fit a prop, motor, ESC or bent arm. The app ranks likely causes; it doesn't diagnose.
  • Battery state of health as a percentage, or remaining capacity. Internal resistance is a sign of ageing, not a capacity measurement.
  • Rotor-frequency or blade-pass vibration on default logs. Default logging is filtered and sampled too slowly to see individual rotors.
  • Per-motor RPM, current or temperature. Default logs don't record them.
  • "Safe to fly" or airworthiness. See section 1.
  • Comparison with other drones or "better than average". Every judgement is relative to your drone or a published guide. The one figure about other drones is context for PX4's vibration guide on your first flights, never a verdict on yours.
  • Wind versus mechanical cause. Wind isn't measured, so a change can't be split between the two.
  • The cause of a change the user didn't record. The app can say when something changed, not why.
  • Absolute tuning quality. Control tracking is only compared with your drone's own history.
  • Payload mass. It isn't logged.
  • Cross-autopilot equivalence. PX4 and ArduPilot numbers are never compared with each other.
  • Anything about simulator fidelity. Comparison with a simulator is planned, not part of the app.
